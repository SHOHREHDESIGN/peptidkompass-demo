GHK-Cu copper peptide: topical research, safety and evidence

GHK-Cu appears in skincare discussions and research-product listings. Those settings need different evidence. Here is what the cited human studies measured, what remains uncertain and how to inspect a supplier’s documents.

What is GHK-Cu?

GHK is the tripeptide glycyl-histidyl-lysine. GHK-Cu refers to its copper complex. Copper-free GHK, GHK-Cu and other modified peptides should not be treated as interchangeable ingredients.

Topical GHK-Cu versus injectable research products

Topical research

A cream or gel is a specific formulation. Its findings apply to the tested formulation and setting, not automatically to every product sharing an ingredient name.

Systemic claims

Skin studies cannot establish the safety or effectiveness of an injected product. A different route changes the question the evidence must answer.

Product identity

Check whether a report measures the peptide, the copper complex or only a broad purity value. Blue colour alone does not authenticate a product.

What do human GHK-Cu studies show?

In a 2006 randomised study after CO2 laser resurfacing, 13 participants completed follow-up. The groups did not differ significantly on the objective redness, wrinkle or overall skin-quality measures. Patient-reported satisfaction was higher in the GHK-Cu group. Subjective satisfaction and objective measurements should therefore be reported separately.

Miller et al. (2006): randomised study

A 2026 evidence-mapping review describes limited, heterogeneous clinical evidence and problems comparing formulations. It distinguishes the biological findings from the question of what chemical form was actually delivered. This is a reason to inspect the original studies rather than generalise from an ingredient name.

A registered topical trial is not a published result

On 1 November 2026, ClinicalTrials.gov listed NCT07437586, a phase 2 study of topical GHK-Cu gel for acute skin wounds, as recruiting. No results were posted. The record’s last posted update was 27 February 2026. These are registry observations, not confirmation of efficacy or actual recruitment progress.

GHK-Cu side effects and safety limits

The evidence discussed here cannot establish a reliable safety profile for injected research products. A lack of reported problems in a small topical study would not exclude uncommon reactions or longer-term risks, and would not validate another formulation.

A laboratory certificate answers selected analytical questions. Identity and purity results do not establish clinical safety, sterility or the absence of endotoxins unless those specific tests were performed and documented.

Questions about copper peptides

Does GHK-Cu grow hair or reverse skin ageing?

Those claims require evidence for a particular formulation and outcome. The limited, mixed clinical evidence discussed here does not support a blanket promise for GHK-Cu products.

Is GHK-Cu the same as a copper supplement?

No. A peptide-copper complex and a generic copper supplement describe different materials. They should not be substituted on the assumption that copper content makes them equivalent.
