Cagrilintide: research, prices and suppliers

A long-acting analogue of the body's own hormone amylin, studied in clinical trials for metabolic and satiety regulation, alone and in combination with semaglutide.

Sources and dates are stated with individual entries. This new presentation is not a complete re-investigation of all data.

FactsEvidenceFrequently asked questionsDocumented pricesQuestions about interpretation

Cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin analogue developed by Novo Nordisk. It is being studied both as a standalone substance and in a fixed combination with the GLP-1 compound semaglutide, marketed under the name CagriSema, in phase 3 trials. To date, cagrilintide is not approved as a standalone medication in any country.

The references below describe different investigations. Cell or animal findings do not establish benefits in humans. A clinical study concerns the tested preparation, population and study design, not automatically a shop product.

Facts

  • CAS number: 1415456-99-3
  • Molecular weight: 4409 g/mol
  • Amino acids: 37
  • Purity specification: ≥ 98 % (HPLC, commercial standard)

Evidence

  • In a randomized phase 2 trial testing several amounts in 706 adults with overweight or obesity, cagrilintide alone over 26 weeks led to a mean body weight loss of 10.8 percent at the highest amount tested, compared with around 3 percent under placebo. Lau DCW et al. 2021, The Lancet 398:2160-2172, PMID 34798060
  • In the phase 3 REDEFINE 1 trial, the cagrilintide-alone arm at 2.4 mg weekly achieved a body weight loss of around 11.8 percent after 68 weeks, compared with 2.3 percent under placebo. Garvey WT et al. 2025, New England Journal of Medicine 393:625-638, PMID 40544433
  • In a diet-induced obesity mouse model, cagrilintide's body-weight-lowering effect depended on the brain amylin receptors AMY1 and AMY3, and the effect was absent in receptor knockout animals. Carvas AO et al. 2025, eBioMedicine, PMID 40609154
  • Structural optimization with fatty-acid lipidation gave cagrilintide a substantially longer half-life of about 159 to 195 hours than the body's own hormone amylin, allowing once-weekly administration in the trial setting. Kruse T et al. 2021, Journal of Medicinal Chemistry 64:11183-11194, PMID 34288673

Frequently asked questions

Is cagrilintide already available as a medication?


No. Cagrilintide is being studied as a standalone substance and in the CagriSema combination in phase 3 trials. An FDA approval process for CagriSema has been underway since December 2025. Cagrilintide is currently not an approved medication.

What sets cagrilintide apart from semaglutide?


Semaglutide acts on the GLP-1 receptor, while cagrilintide acts on amylin receptors in the brain. Both compounds are also studied as a fixed combination called CagriSema, which showed a greater effect on body weight in trials than either substance alone.

Where does cagrilintide come from?


Cagrilintide was developed by the pharmaceutical company Novo Nordisk, which also developed semaglutide. It is referenced under the short designations AM833 and NN9838 in clinical trials, including the REDEFINE trial program.
